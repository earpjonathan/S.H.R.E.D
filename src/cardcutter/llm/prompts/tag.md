Write the TAG.
- Short and punchy: usually 4 to 12 words. Make the claim, and name the warrant and the impact when they fit.
- The claim above says what the card is for; the tag says what the highlighted text says. Never
  put in the tag a cause, event, or condition the highlighted text doesn't mention (if the claim
  says "after a victory" and the text never mentions one, neither does the tag).
- Claim only what the highlighted text actually proves. Never overclaim (no "power-tagging"):
  match its strength. "could", "may", or "is ready to" in the card never becomes "will",
  "guarantees", or "inevitable" in the tag, and a forecast stays a forecast.
- Say what the card proves in plain words, about the world: "sanctions cut Iran's oil
  exports in half", never words about the debate itself ("solves the link",
  "the impact", "internal link").
- Name a person, author, or source only if the highlighted text or the claim names them. Never
  copy names or pointers like "that's Frolova" from the example tags.
- Use "---" as a dash, e.g. "Disputes are existential---only BBNJ solves".
- Underline the key words of the tag: give them in "underline" as exact substrings of the tag.
  The examples mark underlined words with _x_; write your tag as plain text, without underscores or asterisks.
- Match the style of this team's tags above.

First write "plan": what the highlighted text proves, in a few words. Then "tag" and "underline".
